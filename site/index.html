Why local

The bot in your meeting is someone else's server.

Every funded meeting notetaker works the same way. A bot joins the call, or the app uploads your audio, and a vendor's cloud does the transcription. That design has caught up with the industry. Cloud notetaker vendors are now facing wiretap class actions because their servers received meeting audio. Universities blocklist notetaker bots, and Teams can flag or deny them. In one survey, 84 percent of professionals said they self-censor when an AI notetaker joins the call.

Aloe Scribe is built the other way around. The recording, the transcription, the speaker identification, and the summary all happen on your computer. There is no bot, so there is nothing for platforms to flag. There is no upload, so it is structurally impossible for us to see your data. No training on your content, ever.

One honest note. Running locally does not waive recording consent laws. Where the law or your policy requires it, tell people you are recording.

How it works

Press record. Get a finished document.

  1. Start recording

    Aloe Scribe captures your microphone and your computer's system audio on separate channels. It works with Zoom, Teams, Meet, or any app that plays sound, with no bot in the participant list.

  2. Watch the live transcript

    Text appears on screen as people talk, so you know it is working. A notepad sits alongside it for anything you want to jot down.

  3. Tag speakers as they talk

    Click a name once while someone is speaking. Aloe Scribe names everything else that voice says, in this meeting and the next.

  4. Stop, and the transcript lands

    Seconds after you hit stop, a clean Markdown file is on disk with a summary and action items written by a language model that also runs on your machine.

Speaker names

It knows who said what. Acoustically.

Most notetakers guess speakers from the meeting platform's metadata, which falls apart in conference rooms and on phone bridges. Aloe Scribe identifies speakers from the audio itself.

Because the mic and system audio are separate channels, which side of the call a line came from is a fact, not a guess. Within each side, on-device speaker identification tells voices apart. Tag a voice once during the call and majority voting names that voice's entire cluster, past lines included.

Voice fingerprints are stored only on your device, so colleagues you have tagged before are recognized in future meetings without tagging again.

Where your data goes

Audio in. Transcript on disk. Nothing out.

This section doubles as the security one-pager. Show it to your IT or compliance team.

Audio in Your microphone and your computer's own audio output, captured locally. No video is captured.
Processing Transcription, speaker identification, and the summary all run on your CPU and GPU. No audio upload. No cloud transcription.
Storage Open Markdown files with structured metadata, saved to a folder you choose. Voice fingerprints for speaker recognition stay on the device.
Network The only network calls are downloading the app and its models. No accounts. No telemetry on meeting content.

Your transcripts are built to feed your own tools. The Markdown metadata is readable by Glean, Copilot, and anything else you point at the folder. Obsidian vault mode links attendees as wikilinks, so every meeting shows up on each person's page.

Local capture does not waive consent laws. Disclose recording where your jurisdiction or client agreements require it.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is this legal to use?

Aloe Scribe is a recording tool, and recording laws depend on where you and the other participants are. Some places require everyone's consent, others require one party's. Running locally does not change those rules, so disclose that you are recording where the law or your professional obligations require it. When in doubt, ask, the same as you would with any recorder.

Why is there no bot?

A bot exists so a vendor's server can hear your meeting. Aloe Scribe transcribes on your computer, so it does not need one. It captures the audio your computer is already playing and the audio your mic is already hearing. Nothing joins the call, nothing shows up in the participant list, and there is nothing for the meeting platform to flag or block.

Does it work with Teams, Zoom, and Meet?

Yes. Aloe Scribe captures system audio, so it works with any app that plays sound. That includes Teams, Zoom, Meet, Webex, Slack huddles, phone calls routed through your computer, and webinars. It also records in-person meetings with the mic alone.

What does it cost?

It is currently free while in beta. Pricing will be announced. There is no account, so there is nothing to sign up for now and nothing that expires.

Does it need an internet connection?

Only to download the app and its models. After installation, recording, transcription, speaker identification, and summaries all work offline.

What format are the transcripts?

Plain Markdown files with a structured metadata header, saved to a folder you choose. They open in any editor and are built to feed your own AI tools, including Glean, Copilot, and Obsidian. Obsidian vault mode links attendees as wikilinks.

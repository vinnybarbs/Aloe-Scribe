# Copy and structure notes

The hero leads with the positioning line "The private meeting notepad" and immediately backs it with the one claim no competitor can copy, that the privacy is architecture rather than policy. The page then follows a single argument. First the problem section names the market moment (wiretap suits, blocked bots, self-censoring) without naming competitors, since the mechanism is more persuasive than the accusation and carries less legal risk. Then how it works, then the speaker-naming differentiator as its own section because it is the feature proof that local does not mean primitive. "Where your data goes" is written as a four-row table (audio in, processing, storage, network) so a compliance evaluator can screenshot it as the security one-pager, and the consent disclaimer appears twice, once in the why-local section and once under that table, because honesty there builds more trust than it costs.

Structure decisions follow the CRO skill's one-page, one-action rule. There is a single conversion goal, download, with the primary button repeated in the nav, hero, and a dedicated downloads section, and the secondary hero CTA jumps to the data section because privacy proof is this audience's real objection. The transcript mock in the hero shows the actual product output instead of stock imagery, which fits the notepad brand and makes "transcript lands seconds after the meeting ends" concrete. Windows is labeled beta in the requirements card, the download card, and the SmartScreen note, and its missing features are stated twice, since an unsigned 1.4 GB download only converts if expectations are set before the click. The FAQ handles the remaining objections (legality, no bot, platform support, cost) so the page completes the argument without a second page.
